DESCRIPTION (HTML; paste into Zenodo's "Description" rich-text editor)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
<p>This release accompanies the camera-ready manuscript <em>The WKB Geometry of
Newton Polygons: A Functorial Classification Theory</em> and supersedes the v1.0
dataset (<a href="https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20355904">10.5281/zenodo.20355904</a>)
by promoting it to a complete, refereed publication.</p>

<p>For a scalar local linear operator <em>L</em> (differential, difference, or
<em>q</em>-difference) with one irregular singularity at <em>x = &infin;</em>
whose Newton polygon has a unique dominant edge of slope <em>&minus;p/q</em>
with <em>r&thinsp;+&thinsp;1</em> lattice points, the WKB ansatz on that edge
produces a characteristic polynomial &chi; &isin; <b>C</b>[c]. Our central
algebraic identity is &chi;(c) = &chi;<sub>w</sub>(c<sup>q</sup>), where
&chi;<sub>w</sub> &isin; <b>C</b>[w] is the classical edge-indicial polynomial
of degree <em>r</em>. The exponent constellation <em>C(L)</em> &sub; <b>C</b>
is therefore a &mu;<sub>q</sub>-equivariant union of <em>r</em> fibres of
<em>c &rarr; c<sup>q</sup></em> over Roots(&chi;<sub>w</sub>).</p>

<p>This factorisation organises a finite stratified atlas: a computational
sweep yields <b>23 single-edge classes</b> for &minus;p/q &isin;
{1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 2/3, 3/2} and <em>r</em> &le; 3, refining to <b>39 classes</b>
once multi-edge, nested-ramification, and prime-<em>q</em> arithmetic strata
are tracked. We prove a per-edge factorisation along the slope filtration, an
iterated &mu;<sub>q&prime;</sub>-pullback law for nested inner polynomials, a
block-diagonal pullback for systems, and a prime-<em>q</em> Kummer dichotomy
separating the algebraically independent stratum from the cyclotomic stratum.</p>

<p>The analytic input attaching anti-Stokes directions is classical
(Levelt&ndash;Turrittin, Sibuya, Wasow); on the <em>q</em>-fold cover the rays
of a generic single-edge operator satisfy
arg &Delta;<sub>ij</sub> + p&phi; &equiv; &pi;/2 (mod &pi;).
One explicit consequence: the slope numerator <em>p</em>, invisible to
<em>C(L)</em>, is visible to the cover-side ray pattern. The analytic
statements (Theorems AT1, AT3, AT5, AT7) are
&mu;<sub>q</sub>/&mu;<sub>q&prime;</sub>-equivariant repackagings of classical
formal-classification theory.</p>

<p><b>Release contents.</b> Camera-ready LaTeX manuscript (26 pp, 898 KB PDF,
byte-identical compile across all bundles), the full Phase I&ndash;V
computational atlas (19 JSON artifacts, ~2.6 MB), the arXiv-ready and
journal-ready submission bundles, the eight in-manuscript theorem proofs
(M1, N1, D2, S1, AT1, AT3, AT5, AT7), the agent-pipeline scripts
(<code>fleet.py</code> &hellip; <code>fleet5.py</code>), the phase reports,
and the Round-1 + Round-2 refereed revision packages.</p>

release is at
<a href="https://github.com/papanokechi/wkb-newton-polygons">https://github.com/papanokechi/wkb-newton-polygons</a>;
the manuscript points to it from the <em>Reproducibility</em> paragraph of the
Computational and AI Disclosure section.</p>

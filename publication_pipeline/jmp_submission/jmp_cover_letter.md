decomposition, Stokes geometry, and wild character-variety data.

## Significance to mathematical physics

The paper introduces the *constellation functor* `F: L ↦ C(L)` from
operators to their WKB exponent constellations and proves that this
functor is fine enough to detect every datum carried by the wild
character variety on the open generic stratum, while remaining coarse
enough to define a finite classification by 23 explicit cluster classes
indexed by Newton-polygon slope, the inner polynomial χ_w, and a small
list of cyclotomic/Galois invariants.

Three results are likely of immediate interest to readers of *JMP*:

1. **A structural law for the multi-edge case** (`Theorem M1`):
   operators whose Newton polygon has several dominant edges decompose
   functorially as a product of single-edge operators, refining the
   slope decomposition of Levelt–Turrittin–Robba–Malgrange at the
   level of the constellation functor.

2. **A Kummer/cyclotomic dichotomy for the inner polynomial**
   (`Theorem D2`) that controls when the WKB constellation is split by
   the natural action of `μ_q` versus when it is forced into a
   sublattice — phrased in `Z[ζ_q]`-linear and Galois-cohomological
   language compatible with current treatments of wild character
   varieties.

3. **Four analytic-lifting theorems** (`AT1, AT3, AT5, AT7`) that
   transfer discrete invariants (anti-Stokes ray multiset, Δ_ij
   geometry, nested ramification data, p-visibility) to the
   Levelt–Turrittin cover. `AT7` in particular produces an exact
   prediction `2pq(r + q·C(r,2))` for the number of distinct
   anti-Stokes directions per ramified cover that matches the
   numerical Stokes data of the companion atlas at the **single-data-point**
   resolution (verified for `q=5, r=2` across `p ∈ {1,2,3,4}` with
   predicted counts 70, 140, 210, 280, observed counts identical).

The paper is self-contained: definitions, the constellation functor,
proof skeletons, the 23-class taxonomy, and the empirical zoo are all
present in the main text. A computational appendix and the Zenodo
deposit (see below) supply the deterministic pipelines that reproduce
every table and figure.
